# Retrieval / Provenance Layer v2 — current-source refresh

## Why v1 is rejected
The previous retrieval layer relied mainly on the WHO Global Traditional Medicine Strategy (2025–2034) and the 2025 WHO Traditional Medicine Q&A. Those remain valid, but they are no longer the most recent evidence-policy source available as of 26 September 2026.

The retrieval layer is therefore refreshed rather than treated as frozen.

## Current source cards

### R01 — WHO Global research priorities and agenda for TCIM 2025–2034
- Publisher: World Health Organization
- Published: 16 September 2026
- URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240124738
- Use: Current global evidence-gap and research-priority context for traditional, complementary and integrative medicine (TCIM).
- What it supports: WHO identifies misalignment between research activity and evidence needs for policy, safety, service delivery and financing; the agenda sets research priorities and drivers for evidence-informed integration.
- What it does NOT support: efficacy of a specific Ayurvedic, homeopathic, herbal, or other individual intervention.

### R02 — WHO update on the research-priority agenda
- Publisher: World Health Organization
- Published: 17 September 2026
- URL: https://www.who.int/philippines/news/detail-global/17-09-2026-who-calls-for-more-research-into-traditional-medicine-to-meet-growing-health-needs
- Use: Current implementation framing and scale of the 2026 research-priority exercise.
- What it supports: WHO reports the agenda was informed by analysis of 39,927 research grants across 40 funding schemes, 116 research institutes in 67 countries, and consultations involving experts/participants globally; it highlights evidence gaps and research priorities including NCDs and responsible AI.
- What it does NOT support: effectiveness of a particular treatment or a clinical recommendation.

### R03 — WHO Traditional Medicine Q&A
- Publisher: World Health Organization
- Published: 28 November 2025
- URL: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/traditional-medicine
- Use: Evidence, safety and integration boundary.
- What it supports: WHO states TCIM integration should be appropriate, effective and safe, based on the latest scientific evidence; efficacy and safety require rigorous scientific validation.
- What it does NOT support: proof of efficacy for a specific treatment merely because it is traditional or widely used.

### R04 — WHO Global Traditional Medicine Strategy 2025–2034
- Publisher: World Health Organization
- Published: 30 October 2025
- URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240113176
- Use: Strategy-level policy context.
- What it supports: four strategic objectives including strengthening evidence and ensuring safety/regulation.
- What it does NOT support: individual clinical efficacy claims.

## Retrieval rule after refresh
For a substantive clinical/effectiveness claim, these framework sources are insufficient by themselves. The system must retrieve a claim-specific clinical evidence source (for example, a systematic review, guideline, trial, or authoritative clinical reference appropriate to the claim).

Framework sources can support the *evidence boundary*; they cannot be used as citation decoration for a disease-specific or treatment-specific claim.

## Important current finding
WHO's 16–17 September 2026 research-priority publications make the evidence-gap framing more current than the prior source pack. They also explicitly identify responsible AI governance as a cross-cutting issue. This strengthens the product requirement for claim-level provenance and AI-governance controls, but it does not validate any individual health claim.
