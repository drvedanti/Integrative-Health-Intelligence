HEALTH REASONING · PRODUCT CASE STUDY

Make confusing health information easier to reason about.

An evidence-aware reasoning layer that helps people structure a messy health story, explore possible contributors, compare perspectives, understand uncertainty, and leave with better questions for a clinician — without pretending to diagnose.

PRODUCT PROMISE
“What might matter, what does the evidence support, what remains uncertain, and what should I discuss next?”
01 · THE PROBLEM

Health information is abundant. Sense-making is not.

The opportunity is not another symptom checker or generic health chatbot. The harder problem is reasoning across fragmented stories, conflicting explanations, evidence quality and uncertainty.

Fragmented story

Symptoms, triggers, medicines, routines and context often live in different places — and change over time.

Conflicting explanations

People can encounter biomedical, traditional and online explanations without a clear way to distinguish their evidence bases.

Unclear next step

The useful outcome is often not a diagnosis. It is knowing what matters, what is missing and what to ask a clinician.

03 · EVIDENCE MODEL

Different frameworks. Different evidence.

The product does not flatten biomedical and traditional claims into one evidence bucket. Provenance and uncertainty are part of the interface.

Established / stronger support

Guidelines, systematic reviews, clinical studies and other appropriate biomedical evidence.

Limited / mixed

Evidence exists but is inconsistent, indirect, early or insufficient for a confident conclusion.

Traditional framework

Traditional concepts are presented as traditional concepts, not silently converted into biomedical mechanisms.

Unsupported / insufficient

The system should say when the available evidence cannot justify a claim rather than inventing certainty.

Provenance check · PC04

SOURCE FIT: INSUFFICIENT The current WHO 2026 research agenda does not establish efficacy for a specific treatment.

Claim: “WHO's 2026 agenda establishes that a particular herbal product is effective for diabetes.”
Source: R01 — WHO global research priorities and agenda for traditional, complementary and integrative medicine 2025–2034.
Fit: Insufficient for specific treatment efficacy; R01 explicitly cannot support that claim class.
System action: Withhold the efficacy claim and retrieve claim-specific clinical evidence first.
MISSING INFORMATION5 / 9
UNCERTAINTY CHECK

What information could change the picture?

Ask next

When did this start? How often? How long does each episode last? Any sudden onset, weakness, trouble speaking, fainting, fever, vision change or other warning signs?

Useful context

Pregnancy possibility where relevant, current medicines, pain-reliever frequency, sleep pattern and other meaningful changes.

SAFETY ROUTER6 / 9
SAFETY FIRST

Some stories should interrupt exploration.

Example urgent signal
If someone suddenly develops trouble speaking or weakness on one side of the body, the experience should stop the normal reasoning flow and direct them toward urgent emergency care.
Design principle

Safety routing has priority over contributor exploration. The system must not bury red flags beneath educational content.

06 · PRODUCT GUARDRAILS

What the system deliberately refuses to do.

No diagnosis as fact

Possible contributors and uncertainty are not converted into a definitive diagnosis.

No unsafe medication advice

The system does not tell users to stop, start or change prescribed medication.

No false equivalence

Traditional frameworks are clearly separated from biomedical evidence and labelled according to support.

No fabricated sources

Evidence cards need provenance. When evidence is insufficient, the system says so.

No correlation = causation

Temporal patterns are presented as observations, not proof of cause.

Safety before depth

Urgent red flags and mental-health crisis signals interrupt the normal exploration journey.
